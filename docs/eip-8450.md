# EIP-8450, read for distributed validators

[EIP-8450](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8450) (Draft, October 2026, revised 30 October and 5 November;
[discussion](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8450-post-quantum-validator-key-registry/30046)) adds a registry of
post-quantum keys to the beacon state, ahead of the fork that switches duties off BLS. Numbers here come from
`tools/cost_model.py --primary`.

**A second design is in play.** [EIP-8483](pq-fork.md) registers the same material from the withdrawal address
instead, and the consensus-specs `pq` branch builds its emergency fork on it. This page reads EIP-8450 as of
5 November 2026; [`pq-fork.md`](pq-fork.md) reads EIP-8483 and the `pq` branch.

## TL;DR

- **It is the route we argued for.** Two-layer keys (a top tree of 2¹⁶ one-time keys, each certifying a bottom tree
  of 2¹⁶), one key for all duties, a classic hash, signer-chosen leaf indices, and replacement without exit. The
  leanXMSS write-up in leanVM sketched a threshold signer that is our design: PRAWNS-style signing, randomness from a
  threshold PRF, and MPC "only at key generation and once per bottom tree". The EIP now pins EthLib's Lean
  implementation instead, which derives every secret from one seed and has no threshold section.
- **Since 30 October:**
  - each validator also registers a **VRF root** (issue 8);
  - the BLS signing key authorizes registrations and **changes**, at most one per 256 epochs, and the owner path is
    gone;
  - the proof of possession binds the BLS public key, so it can be made before the deposit;
  - builders register too.
- **Registering a DV key is cheap.** Keys are dormant until a later fork, so registration needs only the top layer,
  a proof of possession and a VRF root. By generating a 2¹² top-key range, that's **3–10 GB of MPC per validator per node: 2–7 minutes
  at 200 Mbps**. The full top tree is 36–109 minutes. The VRF tree can cost more than either; its size isn't set
  (issue 8).
- **The biggest DV lever is leaf allocation.** Each bottom tree costs 55–164 GB of MPC. Dense allocation (one leaf per
  signature) needs a tree every 3 days to 5 months, depending on the duty cadence. A fixed allocation of 8 leaves per
  slot, which the consensus-specs `pq` branch uses, needs one every 9–27 hours: up to **128× the MPC**.
- **Our top ask sits outside the EIP: a much higher maximum balance.** Every cost here is per key, so 2¹⁷–2²⁰ ETH
  means 64–512× fewer keys for consolidated stake.
- **Things to fix or clarify for DVs:**
  1. recommend dense allocation;
  2. size the VRF tree, or let its root be renewed (issue 8);
  3. a signer rule for bottom trees stated as an outcome: a top key never certifies two roots;
  4. key-authorized replacement once BLS-authorized changes stop at the switch;
  5. a threshold keystore profile (separate PQ mnemonics are expected in the derivation ERC);
  6. the cost of 2²⁰ grinding trials per signature.
- **Should we build the DKG now? Yes,** scoped to the registered material: top-layer MPC, the proof of possession, the
  VRF tree, and the registration in charon. Bottom trees and per-slot signing can wait until the switch fork defines the
  duties ([§5](#5-what-to-build-now)).

## 1. What the EIP specifies

| | EIP-8450 |
|---|---|
| Key | leanXMSS: two layers of height 16. Top key *b* signs the root of bottom tree *b*, and nothing else. 2³² leaf indices |
| Hash | Keccak-256 truncated to 16 bytes, in leanVM's "cell" encoding |
| One-time key | WOTS+C: 58 chains of positions 0–3, target sum 106, about 2²⁰ randomizer trials per signature |
| Public key / signature | 32 B / **2,404 B**: leaf index + bottom layer (1,200 B) + top layer (1,200 B), on every signature |
| Verification | 186 Keccak-f permutations (93 per layer) |
| Leaf allocation | chosen by the signer; the signature carries its index. Leaf 0 is reserved for the proof of possession |
| Registered material | leanXMSS key (32 B) + **VRF root** (32 B), stored with the epoch of the last change: 72 B per index |
| Registration | a beacon-block operation, 2,580 B: material + proof of possession (an XMSS signature at leaf 0 over `(BLS pubkey, key)`) + BLS authorization; at most 8 per block |
| Authorization | the registrant's **BLS signing key**, for first registrations and **changes**; at most one change per 256 epochs. No owner path |
| Gossip | first-seen per `(index, previous_change_epoch)` |
| Builders | register a leanXMSS key and a zero VRF root |
| Use | none in this fork. A later fork switches duties to the registered keys and has to stop BLS-authorized changes; registered stake is the readiness signal |

**What it chose against our proposal.** We proposed registering each bottom root once ("certified windows", [option
B](two-layer-keys.md#2-two-ways-to-verify)) to keep per-slot signatures at one layer. The EIP instead carries the top
signature in every signature (+944 B and about 1.7× the permutations to prove). Its reason: certifying roots on-chain
adds about sixteen certifications to every block at a million validators. **For DVs the two are equivalent.** The MPC
is identical, and only the signature size differs. We drop it as an ask; a later key version could revisit it.

| our ask ([ranked list](ethereum-design-decisions.md)) | EIP-8450 |

## 3. Issues for distributed validators

### Issue 1: leaf allocation sets the DV's key-generation cost

For a single signer, a bottom tree is about 1.4 × 10⁷ Keccak calls, seconds of CPU. For a DV it's an MPC of 55–164 GB per
node. How fast trees are used up depends entirely on allocation:

| duty cadence and allocation | one bottom tree lasts | average MPC load per validator | validators per node on half of 200 Mbps |
|---|---|---|---|
| today's chain (attest once per epoch, 12 s), dense | ~146 days | 0.04–0.1 Mbps | ~1,000–2,900 |
| per-slot attesting at 12 s, dense | 9.1 days | 0.6–1.7 Mbps | 60–180 |
| per-slot attesting at 4 s (lean), dense | 3.0 days | 1.7–5 Mbps | 20–60 |
| fixed allocation, 8 leaves per slot, 12 s | 27 hours | 4.5–13 Mbps | 7–22 |
| fixed allocation, 8 leaves per slot, 4 s | 9 hours | 13–40 Mbps | 2–7 |

The verifier doesn't care, so this is the signer's choice. **What a DV uses:**
- **Attestations:** synchronized mode (EIP-8310), one leaf per attestation period. The leaf follows from the epoch or
  slot, so the cluster needs no extra agreement.
- **Every other duty:** counter mode in reserved ranges (EIP-8310 §5), one range per duty type. The leaf index rides in
  the duty's QBFT-decided value.

**Ask:**
- name dense allocation as the recommended default in the EIP and in EIP-8310;
- keep the switch fork allocation-agnostic, as this EIP is.

**With decoupled consensus ([EIP-8453](https://github.com/benjaminion/EIPs/blob/645e812ee79729dc955ea45f1597245d4d72b002/EIPS/eip-8453.md), Draft).** Head votes come from a
committee of about 512 validators per slot, chosen with probability proportional to effective balance. Finality
attestations come from every validator, once per finality round.
- **A key's signing rate scales with its balance.**
  - At 2048 ETH, a key sits on the head committee in a few percent of slots.
  - At 2¹⁷ ETH and above, it's on it nearly every slot.
- **Planning case: about one leaf per slot for large keys.** That's a bottom tree every ~3 days at 4 s slots (~9 days
  at 12 s), the "per-slot at 4 s" row above.
- **Per ETH it gets far cheaper.** Signing saturates at one head vote per slot, so a 2²⁰-ETH key costs the same MPC as
  a 2048-ETH key that attests every slot: 512× less per ETH.
- **Allocation:**
  - head votes and finality attestations each get a counter range;
  - large keys can index head votes by slot.
- **Selection proofs now use the VRF tree.** The EIP registers a hash-based VRF root for them (issue 8). Opening a
  leaf is cheap for a DV; building the tree is the cost.

### Issue 2: bottom trees and the seed

The October draft said bottom trees "MUST be derived deterministically from the seed", to stop a regenerated bottom
tree getting a second certification. The current EIP states no rule. EthLib's leanXMSS derives every secret from one
32-byte seed, and the EIP's signer rule covers leaf indices only. A threshold signer has no seed:
- each bottom tree comes from PRF keys generated by MPC for that tree;
- after an operator change, the new operator set generates fresh ones.

**Ask:** when signer rules are written (here or in EIP-8310), state the outcome. A top key MUST NOT certify two
different roots, and a signer MUST durably record a certification before releasing any signature from that tree.
Deterministic derivation is then one way to comply.

Our cluster agrees the root by consensus and keeps a durable "tree *b* certified" record in slashing protection
([two-layer-keys.md](two-layer-keys.md) §4).

### Issue 3: one registration per change epoch

- **First-seen per pair.** Gossip forwards only the first valid registration per `(index, previous_change_epoch)`.
- **The trap is smaller now.** If a key generation is rerun after the cluster has threshold-signed a registration, the
  first one seen can still land. It can be replaced once it's included and 256 epochs (~27 hours) have passed.
- **What we do:** charon signs at most one registration per validator per `previous_change_epoch`, durably recorded,
  like an exit.

### Issue 4: replacement stops at the switch

- **Until the switch, the BLS key replaces keys.** Lost shares, a bad key generation or a version migration need only a
  BLS-authorized change, so withdrawal contracts don't matter. The October owner path is gone.
- **After the switch, nothing.** The EIP says the switch fork "has to stop BLS-authorized changes", since a quantum
  adversary could forge them. A validator whose leanXMSS key or VRF tree then needs replacing would have to exit.
- **Operator changes don't need it.** We reshare the top layer, so the registered key survives.

**Ask:** **key-authorized replacement** at the switch. The registered leanXMSS key signs its successor at a reserved
leaf, rate-limited like today's changes.
- It is post-quantum secure and needs no execution-layer call.
- It covers version migrations, range expiry and a VRF tree that needs renewing (issue 8).

### Issue 5: keystores assume a seed

EIP-8310 encrypts "the XMSS seed", and a draft ERC (ethereum/ERCs#2214) derives the leanXMSS and VRF seeds from the
validator's BLS mnemonic. A DV node holds no seed. Instead it holds:
- top-layer secret shares;
- PRF key shares;
- the bottom tree's public table;
- certification records.

**Offer:** a threshold profile for EIP-8310, written with client teams so that import, export and state-authority rules
stay correct when the key is split.

**Expected:** the draft ERC is expected to allow a PQ mnemonic separate from the BLS one, rather than tying one
secret into two schemes. That suits threshold-generated keys, which have no mnemonic to derive from. Derivation from a
mnemonic should stay optional.

### Issue 6: grinding sits on the critical path after consensus

- **The cost.** A signature takes about 2²⁰ randomizer trials, one permutation each. We measured 0.44 s on one core and
  0.12 s on five (Go `crypto/sha3`, arm64). Optimized multi-lane Keccak or ARM SHA3 instructions would be faster.
- **Why it can't start earlier in a DV.** The base comes from the threshold PRF, so grinding starts only once the
  message is decided and a threshold of PRF shares has arrived. Every node grinds the same deterministic least *i*.
  Splitting the search across nodes would risk two valid ρ for one leaf.
- **Where it bites.** At today's once-per-epoch cadence it's negligible. With per-slot attesting at 4 s, it's about 11
  cores per 100 validators per node, plus ~0.1 s of latency. That is a cost for every large operator, not only DVs.

**Ask:** weigh grinding against verification cost when the switch fork fixes the duty cadence.

### Issue 7: the threshold sketch's top layer needs resharing

- **The sketch** (leanVM `doc/lean_xmss`). Its setup "signs" each bottom root with the top key.
- **The catch for operator changes.** If top-layer secrets come from the same hash-based threshold PRF, they can't be
  reshared without changing the public key.
- **Our design.** We keep the top layer as **Shamir shares**, reshared on operator change, so the registered key never
  moves ([two-layer-keys.md](two-layer-keys.md) §5).

The same holds for VRF secrets drawn from that PRF (issue 8).

**Ask:** a sentence in the sketch noting this.

### Issue 8: the VRF tree

- **What the EIP fixes.** One VRF root per validator: a hash tree whose leaves commit to secrets, opened one index at a
  time. It replaces BLS selection proofs. The root isn't checked at registration, and the fork that uses it defines how
  indices map to duties.
- **The size isn't set.** A change can replace the root until the switch, which has to stop BLS-authorized changes.
  `vrf.Verify` takes a 32-bit index. The consensus-specs `pq` branch gives one
  leaf per slot at depth 31 ([`pq-fork.md`](pq-fork.md)).
- **For a single signer,** it's 2³² Keccak-f in the clear: under an hour of CPU.
- **For a DV,** every leaf hashes a secret, so it's MPC unless the operators share the VRF seed:

  | real leaves (filler elsewhere) | MPC per validator per node | at 200 Mbps |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2³¹: one per 12 s slot, all of them | 10–62 TB | 5–29 days |
  | 2²⁷: 51 years of 12 s slots | 0.6–3.9 TB | 7–43 h |
  | 2²³: 3 years of 12 s slots | 40–242 GB | 27 min–2.7 h |

  1–2 Keccak-f per leaf (`tools/cost_model.py --pq`).
- **The fallback.** Every operator holds the seed and builds the tree in the clear. Any one operator can then predict
  the validator's openings, as a solo staker can its own. That costs a threshold property, not safety.
- **Opening is cheap** either way: a threshold-PRF reconstruction, or one Keccak-f in MPC ahead of time.

**Ask:** let the VRF root be renewed after the switch (issue 4), or give the tree two layers, so threshold signers can
commit a few years at a time. Failing that, keep the index space dense: index by epoch or by a per-duty counter,
not by slot × duty. And say that leaves a validator will never open may be arbitrary.

## 4. Opportunities

- **Registration is risk-free practice.**
  - Registered keys sign nothing in this fork, so DV key generation can run on mainnet with no slashing or liveness
    exposure.
  - The registered-stake readiness signal is where DV-operated stake shows up early, or lags.
- **Keccak suits Boolean MPC.**
  - AND-depth is 24 per permutation, so a whole key generation is about 72 rounds deep against thousands for BLAKE2s.
    WAN latency stops mattering.
  - Per tree it's within about 13% of our BLAKE2s estimate: 174 steps × 38,400 ANDs against 294 × 20,160.
- **The top-range trick makes registration minutes.**
  - Top keys that will never be used can be random filler: past epochs under synchronized allocation, or beyond any
    plausible horizon. A filler leaf has no preimage, so nobody can sign with it.
  - Generating 2¹² real top keys covers ≥ 34 years at 4 s slots (≥ 100 at 12 s) from today's slot, at 1/16 of the cost.
  - Bottom tree 0 only ever signs the proof of possession at leaf 0, so it is one real one-time key plus filler.
- **BLS-authorized changes are a safe upgrade path.** A cluster can register early, then replace the key without
  exiting if parameters or its own tooling change, at most once per 256 epochs, until the switch.
- **The proof of possession names the BLS public key, not the index.** A cluster can make it during the DKG, before
  the deposit, and register as soon as the validator has an index.
- **One key, signer-chosen leaves.** One key for all duties replaces the attestation/proposal pair, halving DV key
  generation, and dense allocation is ours to use.

## 5. What to build now

**Yes, build the DKG, starting with what registration needs.** The circuit is identical to the bottom-tree MPC we
need later: chain walks of Keccak-f on Boolean shares.

| step | what | needs the switch fork's details? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **charon: registration**, BLS-authorized and signed at most once per change epoch (EIP-8450), or material handed to the withdrawal address (EIP-8483) | which registry ships |
| 2 | **Top-layer MPC key generation** and the proof of possession | no |
| 3 | **VRF tree**: shared seed first, MPC once its size is known | the index mapping |
| 4 | **Top-layer resharing** for operator changes over the dormant period | no |
| 5 | Bottom-tree MPC, PRAWNS signing, leaf allocator, grinding | yes: duty set, cadence, aggregation |

**Caveats.**
- **Parameters can still move.** The EIP is a Draft. It now pins leanXMSS to EthLib's Lean implementation at a fixed
  commit, but the draft hash moved from BLAKE2s to Keccak within weeks. Build the MPC parametric in the hash and chain
  shape.
- **Mainnet registration is years out.** It needs the registry fork, which comes after Heze. Until then, the payoff
  is devnets, our input to the spec, and a working ceremony.
- **No dealer keys on mainnet.** A dealer-generated key is known to one machine for life, and it can be replaced only
  until the switch. Registration by MPC costs minutes, so there's no reason to take the shortcut.

## 6. Asks, in order

1. **A much higher maximum effective balance** (outside this EIP). Every cost above is per key; 2¹⁷–2²⁰ ETH means 64–512×
   fewer keys for consolidated stake ([design decisions](ethereum-design-decisions.md#higher-maximum-balance)).
2. **Recommend dense leaf allocation**, and keep the switch fork allocation-agnostic (issue 1).
3. **Size the VRF tree, or let its root be renewed** (issue 8).
4. **Key-authorized replacement** once BLS-authorized changes stop (issue 4).
5. **State the bottom-tree rule as an outcome** (issue 2).
6. **A threshold profile for EIP-8310** (issue 5). Separate PQ mnemonics are expected in the derivation ERC.
7. **Grinding** weighed against verification when the duty cadence is fixed (issue 6).
