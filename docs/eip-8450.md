# EIP-8450, read for distributed validators

[EIP-8450](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8450) (Draft, October 2026;
[discussion](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/eip-8450-post-quantum-validator-key-registry/30046)) adds a registry of
post-quantum validator keys to the beacon state, ahead of the fork that switches duties off BLS. Numbers here come from
`tools/cost_model.py --primary`.

## TL;DR

- **It is the route we argued for.** Two-layer keys (a top tree of 2¹⁶ one-time keys, each certifying a bottom tree
  of 2¹⁶), one key for all duties, a classic hash, signer-chosen leaf indices, and replacement without exit. The
  leanXMSS specification it builds on sketches a threshold signer that is our design: PRAWNS-style signing, randomness
  from a threshold PRF, and MPC "only at key generation and once per bottom tree".
- **Registering a DV key is cheap.** Keys are dormant until a later fork, so registration needs only the top layer and
  a proof of possession. By generating a 2¹² top-key range, that's **3–10 GB of MPC per validator per node: 2–7 minutes
  at 200 Mbps**. The full top tree is 36–109 minutes.
- **The biggest DV lever is leaf allocation.** Each bottom tree costs 55–164 GB of MPC. Dense allocation (one leaf per
  signature) needs a tree every 3 days to 5 months, depending on the duty cadence. The EIP's fixed-allocation example (8
  leaves per slot) needs one every 9–27 hours: up to **128× the MPC**.
- **Our top ask sits outside the EIP: a much higher maximum balance.** Every cost here is per key, so 2¹⁷–2²⁰ ETH
  means 64–512× fewer keys for consolidated stake.
- **Five things to fix or clarify for DVs:**
  1. recommend dense allocation;
  2. state the bottom-tree rule as an outcome, not "derived from the seed";
  3. give contract and immutable withdrawal addresses a way to replace keys;
  4. a threshold keystore profile;
  5. the cost of 2²⁰ grinding trials per signature.
- **Should we build the DKG now? Yes,** scoped to the registered key: top-layer MPC, the proof of possession, and the
  BLS-authorized registration in charon. Bottom trees and per-slot signing can wait until the switch fork defines the
  duties ([§5](#5-what-to-build-now)).

## 1. What the EIP specifies

| | EIP-8450 |
|---|---|
| Key | leanXMSS: two layers of height 16. Top key *b* signs the root of bottom tree *b*, and nothing else. 2³² leaf indices |
| Hash | Keccak-256 truncated to 16 bytes, in leanVM's "cell" encoding |
| One-time key | WOTS+C: 58 chains of positions 0–3, target sum 106, about 2²⁰ randomizer trials per signature |
| Public key / signature | 32 B / **2,404 B**: leaf index + bottom layer (1,200 B) + top layer (1,200 B), on every signature |
| Verification | 186 Keccak-f permutations (93 per layer) |
| Leaf allocation | chosen by the signer; the signature carries its index. Leaf 0 is reserved for the proof of possession |
| Registration | a beacon-block operation: key + proof of possession (an XMSS signature at leaf 0 over `(validator_index, key)`) + authorization |
| Operator path | authorized by the validator's **BLS signing key**; register-only, first-seen on gossip |
| Owner path | authorized by the **withdrawal address** via an EIP-7685 request; can register or **replace**, before and after the switch |
| Use | none in this fork. A later fork switches duties to the registered keys; registered stake is the readiness signal |

**What it chose against our proposal.** We proposed registering each bottom root once ("certified windows", [option
B](two-layer-keys.md#2-two-ways-to-verify)) to keep per-slot signatures at one layer. The EIP instead carries the top
signature in every signature (+944 B and about 1.7× the permutations to prove). Its reason: certifying roots on-chain
adds about sixteen certifications to every block at a million validators. **For DVs the two are equivalent.** The MPC
is identical, and only the signature size differs. We drop it as an ask; a later key version could revisit it.

## 3. Issues for distributed validators

### Issue 1: leaf allocation sets the DV's key-generation cost

For a single signer, a bottom tree is about 1.4 × 10⁷ Keccak calls, seconds of CPU. For a DV it's an MPC of 55–164 GB per
node. How fast trees are used up depends entirely on allocation:

| duty cadence and allocation | one bottom tree lasts | average MPC load per validator | validators per node on half of 200 Mbps |
|---|---|---|---|
| today's chain (attest once per epoch, 12 s), dense | ~146 days | 0.04–0.1 Mbps | ~1,000–2,900 |
| per-slot attesting at 12 s, dense | 9.1 days | 0.6–1.7 Mbps | 60–180 |
| per-slot attesting at 4 s (lean), dense | 3.0 days | 1.7–5 Mbps | 20–60 |
| fixed allocation, 8 leaves per slot, 12 s | 27 hours | 4.5–13 Mbps | 7–22 |
| fixed allocation, 8 leaves per slot, 4 s | 9 hours | 13–40 Mbps | 2–7 |

The verifier doesn't care, so this is the signer's choice. **What a DV uses:**
- **Attestations:** synchronized mode (EIP-8310), one leaf per attestation period. The leaf follows from the epoch or
  slot, so the cluster needs no extra agreement.
- **Every other duty:** counter mode in reserved ranges (EIP-8310 §5), one range per duty type. The leaf index rides in
  the duty's QBFT-decided value.

**Ask:**
- name dense allocation as the recommended default in the EIP and in EIP-8310;
- keep the switch fork allocation-agnostic, as this EIP is.

**With decoupled consensus ([EIP-8453](https://github.com/benjaminion/EIPs/blob/645e812ee79729dc955ea45f1597245d4d72b002/EIPS/eip-8453.md), Draft).** Head votes come from a
committee of about 512 validators per slot, chosen with probability proportional to effective balance. Finality
attestations come from every validator, once per finality round.
- **A key's signing rate scales with its balance.**
  - At 2048 ETH, a key sits on the head committee in a few percent of slots.
  - At 2¹⁷ ETH and above, it's on it nearly every slot.
- **Planning case: about one leaf per slot for large keys.** That's a bottom tree every ~3 days at 4 s slots (~9 days
  at 12 s), the "per-slot at 4 s" row above.
- **Per ETH it gets far cheaper.** Signing saturates at one head vote per slot, so a 2²⁰-ETH key costs the same MPC as
  a 2048-ETH key that attests every slot: 512× less per ETH.
- **Allocation:**
  - head votes and finality attestations each get a counter range;
  - large keys can index head votes by slot.
- **To watch: private selection.** If head committees are selected by a private VRF, a DV must evaluate it as a
  threshold. That needs a post-quantum VRF with a threshold evaluation, and it should be raised early.

### Issue 2: "Bottom trees MUST be derived deterministically from the seed"

The rule protects against a regenerated bottom tree getting a second certification. A threshold signer has no seed:
- each bottom tree comes from PRF keys generated by MPC for that tree;
- after an operator change, the new operator set generates fresh ones.

"Derived from the seed" doesn't describe either case.

**Ask:** state the rule as the outcome it protects. A top key MUST NOT certify two different roots, and a signer MUST
durably record a certification before releasing any signature from that tree. Deterministic derivation is then one
way to comply.

Our cluster agrees the root by consensus and keeps a durable "tree *b* certified" record in slashing protection
([two-layer-keys.md](two-layer-keys.md) §4).

### Issue 3: operator-path registrations are one-shot

- **First-seen wins.** The operator path is register-only, and gossip forwards only the first valid operator-path
  registration per validator.
- **The trap for a DV.** If a key generation is rerun after the cluster has threshold-signed a registration, two signed
  registrations may exist. Either can land, and nothing but the owner path can change it afterwards.
- **What we do:** charon treats signing a registration like an exit, with at most one, durably recorded per validator.

### Issue 4: replacement depends on what the withdrawal address can do

- **The owner path is the only way to replace a key.** Lost shares, a bad key generation, or a version migration all
  go through it.
- **It needs the withdrawal address to call a new predeploy.** Upgradeable contracts and EOAs can. Immutable withdrawal
  contracts without a generic call can't, so their validators can register once and never replace before exiting.
- **Our action:** add PQ key authorization to Obol's validator manager contracts.

**Ask:** consider a third path, **key-authorized replacement**: the registered leanXMSS key signs its successor at a
reserved leaf.
- It is post-quantum secure and needs no execution-layer call.
- It gives every validator rotation for operator changes, parameter-version migrations and range expiry.
- The owner path can still override it.

### Issue 5: keystores assume a seed

EIP-8310 encrypts "the XMSS seed", but a DV node holds none. Instead it holds:
- top-layer secret shares;
- PRF key shares;
- the bottom tree's public table;
- certification records.

**Offer:** a threshold profile for EIP-8310, written with client teams so that import, export and state-authority rules
stay correct when the key is split.

### Issue 6: grinding sits on the critical path after consensus

- **The cost.** A signature takes about 2²⁰ randomizer trials, one permutation each. We measured 0.44 s on one core and
  0.12 s on five (Go `crypto/sha3`, arm64). Optimized multi-lane Keccak or ARM SHA3 instructions would be faster.
- **Why it can't start earlier in a DV.** The base comes from the threshold PRF, so grinding starts only once the
  message is decided and a threshold of PRF shares has arrived. Every node grinds the same deterministic least *i*.
  Splitting the search across nodes would risk two valid ρ for one leaf.
- **Where it bites.** At today's once-per-epoch cadence it's negligible. With per-slot attesting at 4 s, it's about 11
  cores per 100 validators per node, plus ~0.1 s of latency. That is a cost for every large operator, not only DVs.

**Ask:** weigh grinding against verification cost when the switch fork fixes the duty cadence.

### Issue 7: the threshold sketch's top layer needs resharing

- **The sketch.** Its setup "signs" each bottom root with the top key.
- **The catch for operator changes.** If top-layer secrets come from the same hash-based threshold PRF, they can't be
  reshared without changing the public key.
- **Our design.** We keep the top layer as **Shamir shares**, reshared on operator change, so the registered key never
  moves ([two-layer-keys.md](two-layer-keys.md) §5).

**Ask:** a sentence in the sketch noting this.

## 4. Opportunities

- **Registration is risk-free practice.**
  - Registered keys sign nothing in this fork, so DV key generation can run on mainnet with no slashing or liveness
    exposure.
  - The registered-stake readiness signal is where DV-operated stake shows up early, or lags.
- **Keccak suits Boolean MPC.**
  - AND-depth is 24 per permutation, so a whole key generation is about 72 rounds deep against thousands for BLAKE2s.
    WAN latency stops mattering.
  - Per tree it's within about 13% of our BLAKE2s estimate: 174 steps × 38,400 ANDs against 294 × 20,160.
- **The top-range trick makes registration minutes.**
  - Top keys that will never be used can be random filler: past epochs under synchronized allocation, or beyond any
    plausible horizon. A filler leaf has no preimage, so nobody can sign with it.
  - Generating 2¹² real top keys covers ≥ 34 years at 4 s slots (≥ 100 at 12 s) from today's slot, at 1/16 of the cost.
  - Bottom tree 0 only ever signs the proof of possession at leaf 0, so it is one real one-time key plus filler.
- **Owner-path replacement is a safe upgrade path.** A cluster can register early, then replace the key without
  exiting if parameters or its own tooling change. The withdrawal contract has to support it (issue 4).
- **One key, signer-chosen leaves.** One key for all duties replaces the attestation/proposal pair, halving DV key
  generation, and dense allocation is ours to use.

## 5. What to build now

**Yes, build the DKG, starting with what registration needs.** The circuit is identical to the bottom-tree MPC we
need later: chain walks of Keccak-f on Boolean shares.

| step | what | needs the switch fork's details? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **charon: BLS-authorized registration.** Sign `PQKeyRegistration` (genesis-fork domain, as for BLS-to-execution changes), one-shot protection, broadcast. Test with dealer keys on devnets | no |
| 2 | **Top-layer MPC key generation.** Keccak-f in 3–4-party replicated Boolean MPC with PRSS/DN07 preprocessing; 2¹² top range; Shamir shares of chain positions 0–2 stored (11 MB per validator); proof of possession at leaf 0, signed in MPC | no |
| 3 | **Withdrawal-contract support** for the owner path (validator manager contracts) | no |
| 4 | **Top-layer resharing** for operator changes over the dormant period, which may last years | no |
| 5 | Bottom-tree MPC, PRAWNS signing, leaf allocator, grinding | yes: duty set, cadence, aggregation |

**Caveats.**
- **Parameters can still move.** The EIP is a Draft. Its leanXMSS parameters follow a leanVM branch that isn't merged,
  and the draft hash moved from BLAKE2s to Keccak within weeks. Build the MPC parametric in the hash and chain shape.
- **Mainnet registration is years out.** It needs the registry fork, which comes after Heze. Until then, the payoff
  is devnets, our input to the spec, and a working ceremony.
- **No dealer keys on mainnet.** A dealer-generated key is known to one machine for life, and it can be replaced only
  through the owner path. Registration by MPC costs minutes, so there's no reason to take the shortcut.

## 6. Asks, in order

1. **A much higher maximum effective balance** (outside this EIP). Every cost above is per key; 2¹⁷–2²⁰ ETH means 64–512×
   fewer keys for consolidated stake ([design decisions](ethereum-design-decisions.md#higher-maximum-balance)).
2. **Recommend dense leaf allocation**, and keep the switch fork allocation-agnostic (issue 1).
3. **State the bottom-tree rule as an outcome** (issue 2).
4. **Key-authorized replacement** (issue 4).
5. **A threshold profile for EIP-8310** (issue 5).
6. **Grinding** weighed against verification when the duty cadence is fixed (issue 6).
